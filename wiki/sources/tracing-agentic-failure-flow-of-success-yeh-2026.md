# Tracing Agentic Failure from the Flow of Success

**Samuel Yeh, Yiwen Zhu, Shaleen Deep, Sharon Li** (University of Wisconsin–Madison; Microsoft Research) — [arXiv:2607.12747](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12747) (v1, 14 Jul 2026). Preprint; venue not stated.

## Summary

**Failure attribution** — given a failure trajectory, which of its dozens or hundreds of steps caused the failure — is the diagnostic bottleneck for deployed agentic systems. Existing approaches split into two costly families: prompting pipelines that run a frontier LLM over the whole trajectory at inference time, and post-training on failure trajectories with step-level error annotations that are expensive and inherently ambiguous to collect. The authors' reframing is the paper's real contribution: **unsupervised failure attribution**, where the model trains *exclusively on successful trajectories* and localizes error steps in failure trajectories at inference. Successful trajectories are free — every agentic system emits them as a byproduct of normal operation, with zero labeling effort.

**OAT** (One-class Agent Tracing) casts this as one-class learning in latent space. Each step is a mean-pooled last-layer hidden vector from an LLM (Qwen3.5-27B), PCA-projected to 64 dimensions; the sequence of step representations is modeled as a continuous latent path with **Neural Controlled Differential Equations**. At inference, each step of a failure trajectory gets an **anomaly score** equal to its deviation from the dynamics learned on successful runs. Two decoders convert scores to a step set: fixed top-k (k=3), or conformal prediction with a calibrated (1−α)-quantile threshold (α=0.2), which gives a formal false-positive-rate guarantee on in-distribution steps. A **gated control path** — the paper's second design contribution — adaptively suppresses out-of-distribution control signals.

The design choice worth dwelling on is that the model never sees a failure during training. It learns what *normal* looks like, and failure is whatever the flow does not explain. That is what makes the method both label-free and small: a 3-layer MLP vector field, <1 GB VRAM, zero token cost.

## Key points

**In-domain (train and evaluate on MCP-Atlas; 103 successful / 88 failure trajectories, Qwen3.5-27B):**

| Approach | Precision | Recall | F1 | Hit | AUROC | AUPRC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Random-Step | 0.284 | 0.246 | 0.255 | 0.284 | 0.529 | 0.226 |
| First-Step | 0.136 | 0.108 | 0.116 | 0.136 | 0.469 | 0.206 |
| GPT-4o (prompted) | 0.233 | 0.210 | 0.212 | 0.250 | 0.509 | 0.217 |
| GPT-5 (prompted) | 0.217 | 0.183 | 0.181 | 0.250 | 0.515 | 0.217 |
| **OAT (Top-k)** | 0.321 | **0.706** | 0.420 | **0.777** | **0.629** | **0.324** |
| **OAT (CP)** | **0.443** | 0.484 | **0.435** | 0.566 | — | — |

**OOD (train on MCP-Atlas, evaluate on Who&When; 184 annotated failure trajectories from GPT-4o):** OAT (Top-k) F1 **0.225** / AUROC 0.758 vs. GPT-5 0.152 and GPT-4o 0.151. This is a hard shift — MCP-Atlas is single-agent tool calling generated by Qwen3.5-27B, Who&When is multi-agent collaboration generated by GPT-4o.

- **Reading the abstract's "+20% and +7% F1":** these are point gains over the best **prompting** baseline, and they track OAT (Top-k): 0.420 − 0.212 = +20.8 pp in-domain, 0.225 − 0.152 = +7.3 pp OOD. The paired AUPRC claims (+10%, +5%) check out the same way.
- **The result that should give pause:** in-domain, the trivial **Random-Step baseline (F1 0.255) beats both GPT-4o (0.212) and GPT-5 (0.181)**, and GPT-5 is worse than GPT-4o. Frontier-model prompting is at or below chance-level single-step guessing on this task — consistent with Zhang et al.'s original finding that SOTA reasoning models score below 15% accuracy on error-step localization. (Note the baselines select only one step, so precision/recall are not strictly apples-to-apples.)
- **Cost:** OAT emits **0 output tokens** with **7 ms** latency on MCP-Atlas and **16 ms** on Who&When, vs. GPT-4o at 4,241 ms / 4,522 ms and GPT-5 at 39,626 ms / 37,819 ms — the basis for the **200–5000×** speedup claim. Deployment needs <1 GB VRAM.
- **Ablations isolate each design choice:** Neural CDE beats Neural ODE across all metrics (the control path, which continuously re-injects observations into the latent dynamics, matters); Neural CDE beats an RNN one-class baseline predicting the next hidden state; the **gated control path buys +0.172 AUROC OOD at a cost of −0.028 in-domain** — an asymmetry the authors read as the gate purchasing generalization; later-layer representations (of Qwen3.5-27B's 64 layers) consistently beat earlier ones.
- **Decoder trade-off is clean:** top-k over-detects (recall 0.706, hit 0.777, precision 0.321); conformal prediction is calibrated and balanced (precision 0.443, F1 0.435).
- **Qualitative behavior:** OAT scores hallucinations, unfaithful assumptions, and code bugs highly while keeping benign steps low. In cases where it misses the annotated root cause, it still assigns progressively rising scores to the suboptimal plan preceding the failure and reliably fires where the failure becomes explicitly verbalized.
- **Practical corollary of being unsupervised:** adapting to a new domain means collecting more *successful* trajectories there — no annotation round needed before deployment.
- **Caveat not foregrounded by the authors:** OAT needs white-box hidden states from a proxy model (here the same Qwen3.5-27B that generated the trajectories), so it is not a drop-in for purely closed-API agent stacks. The stated limitations section discusses only method extensions (continuous normalizing flows for exact-likelihood anomaly scores; attention-based control paths).

## Concepts & entities

- [[agent-evaluation]] — failure attribution is the post-hoc diagnostic layer beneath aggregate benchmark scores; this paper makes it cheap enough to run on every failed trajectory rather than on sampled ones.
- [[agent-as-a-judge]] — the baseline paradigm being displaced. Prompting a frontier model to read a trajectory and name the faulty step is exactly agent-as-judge, and here it loses to a 3-layer MLP that never reads text.
- [[verifier]] — an unusual point in the verification design space: a *learned, training-free-of-labels* verifier operating on latent dynamics rather than on natural-language rubrics, with a conformal FPR guarantee instead of a calibrated score.
- [[hallucination-detection]] — the worked case study in Figure 2 is a hallucination at step 7 plus its propagated consequence at step 9, detected as anomalies rather than by fact-checking.
- [[agentic-harness-engineering]] — supplies the missing telemetry primitive: harness-evolution loops need to know *which step* broke, and doing that at 7 ms per trajectory makes it viable inside an optimization loop.
- [[reflexion]] — self-critique loops presuppose that the agent can localize its own error; OAT provides that localization externally and without the failing model's own (possibly rationalized) account of what went wrong.
