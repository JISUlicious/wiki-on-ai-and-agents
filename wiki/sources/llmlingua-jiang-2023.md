# LLMLingua: Compressing Prompts for Accelerated Inference of Large Language Models

**Huiqiang Jiang, Qianhui Wu, Chin-Yew Lin, Yuqing Yang, Lili Qiu** (Microsoft Corporation) — [arXiv:2310.05736](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05736), EMNLP 2023.

## Summary

Where caching methods avoid *re-doing* prefill, LLMLingua attacks the other side of the problem: send **fewer tokens into prefill in the first place**. It is a coarse-to-fine **prompt compression** method that drops tokens from a prompt using a *small* language model's perplexity signal, on the premise (from information theory) that low-perplexity tokens contribute little entropy and can be removed with minor effect on the target LLM's comprehension. Crucially it requires no gradient flow through the target LLM, so it works against black-box API models like GPT-3.5-Turbo and Claude-v1.3.

Three components. (1) A **budget controller** allocates different compression rates across the prompt's parts — instruction, demonstrations, question — protecting instruction and question (τ_ins = 0.85, τ_que = 0.9) while compressing demonstrations hard, and performs coarse-grained demonstration-level dropping first so semantic integrity survives at high ratios. (2) **Iterative Token-level Prompt Compression (ITPC)** does the fine-grained pass: rather than scoring every token independently (the flaw the authors attribute to Selective-Context), it processes the prompt in segments of 100 tokens and conditions each segment's perplexities on the *already-compressed* prefix, so interdependence between retained tokens is modeled. (3) **Distribution alignment** instruction-tunes the small LM (Alpaca-7B or a GPT2-Alpaca) on data generated by the target LLM, closing the gap between the compressor's distribution and the target's.

The compression step is not free — it costs forward passes through a *small* LM (not the target LLM), and the paper accounts for this explicitly. Their cost model (Eq. 9) has total compute `(L + kL/τ + L/τ)·c_small + (L/τ)·c_LLM`; with c_small ≈ 7/175 ≈ 1/25 of c_LLM and 5× compression, total compute is ≈ 0.264·L·c_LLM, a **net ~4× saving**. The measured end-to-end latency numbers below are likewise net of the compression pass, so the speedups are real, not accounting artifacts. Compression does trade accuracy for tokens, but the trade is remarkably cheap up to ~20× and then falls off a cliff.

## Key points

- **Headline: up to 20× compression with ~1.5 points of performance loss.** On GSM8K (GPT-3.5-Turbo, greedy decoding) the full-shot prompt scores 78.85 EM with 2,366 tokens. LLMLingua scores **79.08 EM at 446 tokens (5×) — slightly better than full-shot** — then 77.41 at 171 tokens (14×) and **77.33 at 117 tokens (20×)**, i.e. drops of 1.44 and 1.52 points.
- **Beats every compression baseline by a wide margin on reasoning.** At 20× on GSM8K, Selective-Context scores 44.20 versus LLMLingua's 77.33 — a **33.10-point gap**. GPT-4-generated compression manages only 56.33 at 20×, and sentence selection 66.67 at 12×. The authors argue phrase-level self-information and free-form LLM rewriting both sever chain-of-thought reasoning chains.
- **BBH is harder.** Full-shot 70.07 EM; LLMLingua 70.11 at 3× (1-shot constraint), then **−8.5 points at 5×** and **−13.2 points at 7×**, with the loss concentrated on difficult tasks such as `tracking_shuffled_objects_seven_objects`. So the "20× nearly free" headline is dataset-dependent.
- **Contextual understanding**: on ShareGPT (conversation) and Arxiv-March23 (summarization), compression ratios of roughly 3.3× and 9× are reached while preserving high BERTScore F1 (~89–90), outscoring sentence selection and Selective-Context on BLEU/ROUGE at matched budgets.
- **Latency, net of compression (Table 6, GSM8K, V100-32G):** end-to-end **8.6 s uncompressed → 4.9 s at 2× (1.7×), 2.3 s at 5× (3.3×), 1.3 s at 10× (5.7×)**. LLMLingua's own compression pass accounts for only 0.8 s / 0.3 s / 0.2 s of those totals. The overhead shrinks as the ratio rises because coarse demonstration-level dropping happens first, leaving less text for the token-level pass.
- **Dollar cost (GPT-3.5-Turbo pricing, Table 7):** $5.2 → $0.5 on GSM8K, $12.8 → $4.8 on BBH, $0.7 → $0.3 on ShareGPT, $1.3 → $0.2 on Arxiv — savings of $4.7 / $8.0 / $0.4 / $0.8.
- **Secondary saving in decode.** Higher compression ratios also *shorten generated output*, so the win is not confined to prefill.
- **A GPT2-small compressor almost works.** With GPT2-Alpaca instead of Alpaca-7B: 77.02 EM at 5×, 76.42 at 14×, 76.27 at 18× — a 0.99–2.06 point drop versus the 7B compressor, at far lower compression cost.
- **Failure mode.** Beyond roughly **25×–30× on GSM8K all methods, including LLMLingua, degrade sharply**; the authors flag this as a limitation.
- **Curiosity worth noting**: GPT-4 can largely *reconstruct* the original prompt from LLMLingua's compressed text (a 17× compressed GSM8K prompt was restored to its full 9-step reasoning chain), while GPT-3.5-Turbo cannot — the authors suggest decompression is an emergent ability.

## Concepts & entities

- [[prompt-compression]] — the technique introduced here: drop low-perplexity tokens with a small LM before the prompt ever reaches the target model's prefill.
- [[in-context-learning]] — demonstrations are the main compression target; the budget controller protects instruction and question while shrinking the exemplar block.
- [[chain-of-thought]] — GSM8K/BBH CoT prompts are the hardest case, and preserving reasoning steps is exactly where LLMLingua beats phrase-level and GPT-4-rewrite baselines.
- [[long-context-llm]] — motivated by prompts reaching tens of thousands of tokens; the authors frame compression as a way to fit more context into a fixed window.
- [[kv-cache]] — named in the conclusion as a further target: compressing the KV cache itself to improve inference efficiency.
- [[quantization-performance]] — positioned as the alternative efficiency lever that LLMLingua deliberately avoids, since weight-level methods are unavailable for API-only models.
- [[prefix-caching]] — complementary rather than competing: caching reuses prefill work, compression reduces how much prefill work exists.
