# ELIZA (Weizenbaum, 1966)

Joseph Weizenbaum, *Communications of the ACM* 9(1), January 1966, pp. 36–45. Introduces [[eliza|ELIZA]], a keyword-and-transformation-rule program that holds a typed conversation according to a replaceable script. Its best-known script imitates a Rogerian psychotherapist.

## Key points

- The program has no model of the world. Scripts supply decomposition and reassembly rules for keywords.
- The therapist setting is chosen because that kind of conversation lets the program get away with knowing almost nothing.
- Weizenbaum's concern is the illusion of understanding users form, which he treats as a finding of the work.

## Influence

[[generative-agents-park-2023|Generative Agents]] (2023, p. 3) names ELIZA and SHRDLU as formative work showing both the opportunities and the risks of natural-language interaction. See [[ai-agent-influence-graph]].

> [!note] Not an agent in the later sense
> ELIZA converses; it does not act on an environment. Its link to agents is through conversation and anthropomorphism, not through the agent loop.
