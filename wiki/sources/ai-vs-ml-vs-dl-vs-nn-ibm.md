# AI vs. Machine Learning vs. Deep Learning vs. Neural Networks (IBM)

**Source**: `sources/ai-ml-dl-nn-ibm.md` ([ibm.com/think](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/ai-vs-machine-learning-vs-deep-learning-vs-neural-networks), fetched 2026-10-06)
**Author / date**: IBM Think explainer. No publication date could be extracted; treat it as the 2026 live version.

## Summary

IBM's version of the nested-fields picture:
- "AI is the overarching system."
- "Machine learning is a subset of AI."
- "Deep learning is a subfield of machine learning."
- "Neural networks make up the backbone of deep learning algorithms."

It is "the number of node layers, or depth, of neural networks that distinguishes a single neural network from a deep learning algorithm."

## Key definitions

- **Machine learning:** a subset of AI that learns to predict, minimizing the errors of "merely guessing". Example: product recommendations.
  - *Classical* ML depends on human experts to choose the features that distinguish classes.
- **Deep learning:** can ingest unstructured data such as raw text and images, and "automatically determine the set of features". It uses labelled data when available, but doesn't necessarily require it.
- **Neural networks:** an input layer, one or more hidden layers and an output layer of nodes, each with weights and a threshold.
  - "A neural network of more than three layers, including the inputs and the output, can be considered a deep-learning algorithm."
- **Generative AI:** "foundation models that train on large amounts of unlabeled data", adaptable to new uses. This is the closest the page comes to naming LLMs.
